# Posts

Copy for the @ponsnipe X account. One post per block; anything marked
**thread** is meant to go out as numbered replies.

Two rules held throughout, both learned from the first draft being too much:

- **Say what it does for them, not how it works.** Nobody picks a tool because
  of its database schema. The tech posts below are the exception, and they
  exist to answer "is this real", not to explain the build.
- **Never imply a return.** Everything here describes what the tool does. None
  of it says anyone will make money, because we do not know that and it is the
  fastest way to deserve a reply nobody wants.

The numbers used are ones we measured on Robinhood Chain, not estimates. If a
number is quoted in a post it is in `docs/FINDINGS.md` or was measured live.

## The tech, in plain words

> "How is it faster than me?"
>
> It is not watching a website. It is reading the chain directly, and it never
> blinks.
>
> A new coin is in the feed about a tenth of a second after it exists.

---

> Every chart you have seen for a new coin is drawn from a price someone
> sampled every few seconds.
>
> Ours is drawn from every single trade.
>
> That is the difference between a smooth line and the wick that took your
> stop out.

---

> Robinhood Chain makes a block every 105 milliseconds.
>
> So we made the candles 200 milliseconds.
>
> On a coin that lives three minutes, a one-minute candle is the whole story
> in three boxes.

---

> Your keys stay in your browser. Encrypted, on your machine, never sent
> anywhere.
>
> We cannot move your money. That is on purpose.

## Filters and the feed

> Half of all pons launches never trade even once.
>
> You should not have to scroll past them. So now you do not — filter them
> out and never see them again.

---

> Filter new pairs by market cap, volume, liquidity, holders, buys, sells,
> dev holdings, snipers, bundled wallets and more.
>
> Hide the bundled ones. Hide the ones the dev already sold.
>
> Then look at what is left.

---

> One number we will not put on the screen: a "safety score".
>
> We do not check contracts for honeypots, so we do not have a box for it. A
> tick that never fails is worse than no tick — it tells you something was
> checked when nothing was.
